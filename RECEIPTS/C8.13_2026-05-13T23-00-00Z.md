### Self-check

**What could I have gotten wrong that VERIFY wouldn't catch?**

1. **Disk-cache effects on the measurement.** Both pipelines re-ran on a machine where the prior shipped parquets already existed in OS page cache. The parse stage reads raw zips (uncacheable at scale; ~3.6 GB combined; not cache-amenable) but harmonize + derive may have benefited from cached intermediate parquets. A cold-cache run would likely add 0-5% to harmonize + derive. **Mitigation**: documented as Caveat 2 in `docs/PIPELINE_TIMING_BENCHMARK.md`; the natality result is so close to ~90 min (-1.72%) that even a +5% cold-cache penalty leaves it within PASS; the fetal-death result is more sensitive but the PROPOSE-EDIT is bidirectionally robust.

2. **Build-machine vs manuscript "2024-vintage laptop" anchor.** The reference machine is editorial framing, not a precise hardware spec. A factor-of-2 hardware spread can move per-stage wall-clock by ±50%. **Mitigation**: ±10% VERIFY tolerance is hardware-tolerant for moderate spreads; the manuscript anchor remains editorial; documented as Caveat 1.

3. **Validate stages excluded.** The manuscript's "pipeline runs end-to-end" framing was interpreted as parse → derive primary chain (validate is Level-2 verification framing). If a reader interpreted the claim as including validate, the natality total would shift higher by ~few minutes (the natality validators chain is non-trivial). **Mitigation**: documented as Caveat 3; the natality -1.72% drift has headroom.

4. **The 43-year fetal-death measurement vs the stale `run_pipeline.py` ALL_YEARS=29 envelope.** The manuscript's "~6 min" was likely authored when the canonical pipeline covered 29 years; the 43-year envelope is post-V3a/V3b/V2.1/C8.2. The driver explicitly enumerates 43 years; the measurement reflects current state. If the manuscript line is read with the historical-29-year context, the -13.1% drift overstates. **Mitigation**: the manuscript text doesn't pin a year count; the reasonable reading is current-state envelope. Documented as Caveat 5.

5. **Sequential vs parallel pipelines.** Manuscript wording implies serial timing for each pipeline; a user with a multi-core machine could run them concurrently. **Mitigation**: documented as Caveat 4; sequential is the conservative claim.

6. **PROPOSE-EDIT framing assumes Phase D step 4 will revisit.** If Phase D step 4's manuscript re-pass somehow skips this line, the recommendation goes unactioned. **Mitigation**: the Phase D step 4 framing in KICKOFF.md says *"Update all numerics affected by Phase B/C work"* — a -13.1% drift is exactly the kind of numeric that would surface at re-pass; the PROPOSE-EDIT is a forward-looking HALT for Phase D step 4 reading this RECEIPT.

7. **H10 byte-exactness could mask non-deterministic compute paths.** The SHA equality after re-derive proves output byte-equality; it does NOT prove the internal compute path is deterministic (e.g., a downstream RNG seeded by wall-clock that happens to produce the same output bytes due to deterministic-by-coincidence). **Mitigation**: the natality + fetal-death pipelines use pandas + pyarrow with no RNG; deterministic by design; H10 byte-equality is a sufficient gate.

8. **The natality `parse_all_v1_years.py` and `parse_all_linked_years.py` may have their own internal year-loop logic that doesn't precisely mirror "one parse per zip" timing.** Per-year breakdown within these batches is not surfaced in the F.5 CSV (only the batch wall-clock is). **Mitigation**: the manuscript claim is per-pipeline (not per-year for natality), so batch-level timing is the right granularity for VERIFY.
